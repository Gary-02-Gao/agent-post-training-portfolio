# Research judgment and correction

These cases summarize the project author's decisions during the research. They are editorial accounts, not transcripts or raw experimental records.

## 1. Separate an accurate answer from a correct action

Some model runs attempted a repeated action, were stopped by the tool, and then gave an accurate final response. The author retained the operational failure while also recording that the protection prevented an actual incorrect refund.

This separated three questions: was the reply accurate, was the attempted action justified, and did the environment suffer an incorrect state change? Training decisions should not be based on only one of these observations.

## 2. Control the comparison before explaining the improvement

The first history-intervention comparison showed a favorable difference, but its tool responses were serialized differently across model conditions. The author accepted the individual task verdicts while withholding the stronger training-only attribution.

A controlled retest used the existing checkpoints with aligned actual inputs. The stopping difference persisted. This strengthened the bounded finding without establishing a single internal cause or converting local next-action tests into complete-task results.

## 3. Accept the stronger simple baseline

The diagnostic intervention had already succeeded against an earlier control. The author nevertheless supported an independently constructed conventional scheme with enough common facts and resources to be competitive.

The conventional scheme better satisfied the new batch's predeclared operation contract. The author selected it, preserved the negative result for the diagnostic scheme and rejected the idea of selecting favorable follow-up tasks simply to rescue the original explanation.

## 4. Distinguish hidden truth from what the model observed

In a later task, an independent source could have resolved the request, but the model did not consult it. The author distinguished failure to obtain a fact from ignoring a fact that had already been observed.

The interface also left an action priority unclear. Rather than immediately treating the failure as a training need, the project tested a same-model interface change and kept simple system assistance as a comparator. A bounded negative result for one interface change did not establish that all engineering alternatives were ineffective.

## 5. Improve evaluation without rewriting prior results

A missing source record and an unfinished query are different evidence states. The author required semantic evaluation to reflect that distinction, and required a full query chain rather than a final-page indicator alone to establish completion.

The revised standard was frozen for later evaluation. Earlier judgments retained their original scope instead of being rewritten to produce a more uniform historical score.

## The research position

The framework supports evidence-based investigation, and a specific SFT intervention produced a controlled positive result. A later competitive comparison did not show added benefit for the diagnostic design.

The author treats both findings as useful: research judgment includes deciding when to retain a simpler approach, defer training or revise an explanation. More engineering and more training do not by themselves establish a stronger research contribution.
